A plain-words story of NEW business only (renewals are left out). Built from Salesforce leads, Distribution Engine logs, opportunities, and lender submissions on September 10, 2026. Wherever you see 2026*, it means January–August 2026.

The short version

Five things to know

The words we use. A lead is a business that raised its hand and asked about money. Assigned means the Distribution Engine handed that lead to a salesperson. An opportunity (opp) means the salesperson got the paperwork and sent the deal to lenders. Approved means at least one lender said yes. Funded means the money actually went out the door. SBG revenue is what SBG earns on a funded deal.
  1. The machine got about 3½ times bigger. Compared with 2023, SBG now has 3.6x the salespeople working new leads, hands out 3.3x the leads, funds 3.3x the deals, and sends out 3.8x the dollars every month.
  2. Each salesperson does about the same as in 2023. A rep funded 9.0 deals a month in 2023 and 9.3 in 2026, worth about $40.5K then and $43.6K now in SBG revenue. Growth came from adding people and leads, not from each person getting better.
  3. The funnel leaks more at the top and the bottom. A smaller share of new leads gets assigned (74% → 60%). Lenders approve more deals (53% → 59%), but fewer approvals turn into money (30% → 26%). The loss moved to after the lender says yes.
  4. Deals got bigger, SBG’s slice got thinner. The average funded deal grew from $48.7K to $57.2K, mostly thanks to Sofi referrals and bigger-business lead tiers. But SBG revenue per funded dollar slipped from 9.0% to 8.0%.
  5. The biggest levers are clear. (1) Fix the after-approval leak: closed-lost now eats 62% of approvals and “lost contact with merchant” has grown 4x. (2) Rebalance lead buying: businessloans.com conversion fell from 26% to 14% while Sofi, Bluevine and On Deck referrals convert 2–3x better. (3) Get new-hire teams productive faster; the managers running mostly new reps produce 25–45% less per rep.

Two ways to count “assigned”: the Distribution Engine logs (true hand-offs, one business counted once per month) and the lead report (leads that were ever assigned). The log number is the one to trust; the gap between them grew because more leads now get re-routed as duplicates.

What it means. Lenders say yes more often than they used to (a real win, driven by the Sofi and bigger-business mix). But the share of approved deals that actually fund has slipped every single year, and the share of approvals ending as “closed lost” climbed from 52% to 62%. Overall, close on submissions is basically flat at 15–16%, so the whole funnel from lead to money is the same as 2023, just much bigger.

How leads get handed out

From three round-robin teams to size-based tiers plus partner queues

The Distribution Engine decides who gets each lead. Here is how that changed. Share = share of unique assigned leads; conversion = lead to opportunity.
  • 2023: Three round-robin teams (A, B, C) took 71% of leads. Another 13% were “attempting to contact” leads recycled to a second rep (they converted only 11%). Leads were re-assigned 1.27 times each on average.
  • 2024: Teams A/B/C became Teams 1–6, which are lead tiers by business size, not manager teams (the same 95 reps are members of Teams 2–7). A new VSS phone team took 28% of leads (small businesses) and the Sofi partner queue started.
  • 2025: Round-robin tiers took 89% of leads, Sofi 7%; VSS was wound down to almost nothing; ATC recycling stopped.
  • 2026: Round-robin 83%, Sofi 9%, Bluevine queue added, ATC redistribution brought back (3% of leads, converting 6%), and a “Redistribution” flag now marks 5% of all log entries. Reassignments per lead ticked up to 1.08x.

The verdict

Strengths, weaknesses, trends, and problems

Strengths

  • Scale: 3.3x the funded deals and 3.8x the dollars of 2023, every month.
  • Lender approval rate up from 53% to 59%; lender base is wide (40+ funding, 50 receiving submissions).
  • Deal size up 17% to $57K; the $100K+ share of fundings rose from 12% to 18%.
  • Partner sources (Sofi, Bluevine, On Deck, merchant referrals) convert 2–3x better than bought leads and are growing.
  • Smarter routing: size tiers put the biggest businesses (32% of leads) with converting reps.
  • Less dependence on stars: the top 20% of reps carry 35% of fundings, down from 49%.

Weaknesses

  • No productivity gain per rep in four years: about 9 funded and $43K revenue per AE per month, flat.
  • Close on approvals fell every year (30% → 26%); closed-lost now eats 62% of approvals.
  • Bought-lead quality is falling: businessloans.com and Fundera lead-to-opp roughly halved; sub-580 credit is 31% of opps.
  • SBG’s take per funded dollar fell from 9.05% to 8.04%.
  • Only 60% of created leads reach a new-business rep (was 74%).
  • Win-back and deal-lost recycling is essentially unused (about 1% of fundings).

Emerging trends

  • Revenue-share leads went from 15% to 49% of opps and close better than paid leads.
  • Headcount is now outrunning leads: AEs up 34% on 2025, assigned leads up 6%; leads per rep fell under 200 in mid-2026.
  • Submissions per deal climbed from 8 to 12; lender mix shifting to On Deck / Forward / Expansion; Headway faded out.
  • Longer tails: average lead-to-funded time doubled even though typical deals are as fast as ever.
  • Bigger tiers and bigger products (term loans up to 16% of fundings) are pushing deal size up.

Problems to fix first

  • The post-approval leak. “Lost contact” is 4x 2023 and “did not like the approval” is 44% of losses. Set a same-day-offer rule, track time from approval to first contact, and give new reps scripted offer presentation.
  • New-rep ramp. Reps under 3 months close 14% vs 17–18% for veterans, and the three new-hire managers run 25–45% below the pack. Ramp is the manager gap.
  • Lead buying. Re-price businessloans.com and Fundera against $47–65 revenue per lead; shift budget toward Sofi/Bluevine-type partnerships worth $120–565 per lead.
  • Low-tier leads. Sub-580 credit, under-$200K revenue and Tier 1 leads close 5–9%. Route them to a cheaper path (VSS-style or automated) instead of AEs.
  • Headcount pacing. Tie hiring to assigned-lead growth; at today’s lead flow, more reps mostly means fewer leads each.
Data notes

How the numbers were built

  • Period. Calendar years 2023, 2024, 2025 and January–August 2026. Every yearly figure is the monthly average for that window; September 2026 (partial) is excluded.
  • New business only. Opportunities with Type = New. Leads routed to the Renewals distribution team (about 18–19K unique per year in 2025–26) are excluded from assigned counts. Leads created cannot be perfectly split, so about 5% of 2025–26 “leads created” are existing customers.
  • Leads assigned. From Distribution Engine logs (n2de__Distribution_log__c), counting each lead once per calendar month, with the Renewals team excluded. The lead-report “ever assigned” share is shown separately for comparison.
  • Funnel rates. Approved = Approved flag, a Pre-Approved date, or a stage of Pre-Approved / Contracts / Funded. Funded = Funded stage with a funded date; funded dollars use Funded Amount; SBG revenue uses the SBG Revenue field. Approval bands use Largest Approval (Max Approval is empty on every record).
  • Headcount. AEs = users with the Sales Rep or Sales Rep - Manager profile who received at least 10 unique leads in the month. VSS = Sales Rep - VSS profile.
  • Managers. The Distribution Engine’s Assigned User Manager stamp exists from November 2024. Each rep-month is credited to the manager who received most of that rep’s leads that month; deals are credited by the opp’s creation month. Adam Block (VSS and onboarding pool), Jake Rotman (VP pool), and ops users are shown but not ranked. 2023 has only Team Jake / Team Adam stamps.
  • Distribution Teams 1–7 are lead tiers (the same reps are members of every tier), not manager teams.
  • Sales cycle. Lead assigned → opp uses the opp’s first-assigned date to its created date; opp → funded uses created to funded date. Medians are the “typical” deal; means include the long tail.
  • Seasonality. Average of each month’s share of its own year’s total, 2023–2025.
  • Win-back. Funded deal whose account had an earlier new opp ending Closed Lost or Declined (2021 onward).
